AI can help you reach more people. It can also misdescribe a sacred object to thousands of visitors before anyone notices.

I help museums, archives, libraries, and heritage sites use AI in collections work and visitor experience without putting scholarly accuracy, provenance, or public trust at risk.

Consulting program

Build a strategic plan

Set your direction for the next three to five years with a plan grounded in what your communities, staff and funders actually need, and one your team can use to make everyday decisions.

Request a tailored proposal
Format
Facilitated engagement, on-site and remote
Duration
3 to 6 months
Participants
Director, board and senior leadership, with input from staff and community partners
What you get
  • A strategic plan with clear priorities, owners and measures
  • A plain-language summary for your board and funders
  • A simple rhythm for reviewing progress each year

The program includes

  1. Listen first: interviews with leadership, staff, board and key partners, alongside a review of your audience data.
  2. Read the landscape: funding, visitor trends, technology and regulation, including where AI fits and where it doesn’t.
  3. Set priorities with your leadership team in facilitated working sessions.
  4. Draft the plan with goals, owners and measures that are realistic for your resources.
  5. Present and hand over to your board, with a light process for checking progress each year.

Research

Community listening

Find out what your communities want to talk about, see and take part in, before you commit budget to new programming.

Request a tailored proposal
Format
Listening sessions and interviews, in person where it matters
Duration
6 to 10 weeks
Participants
Visitors, non-visitors, community groups and the partners you want to reach
What you get
  • A findings report in plain language
  • Programming recommendations tied to what you heard
  • A plan to report back to the people who took part

The program includes

  1. Agree what you need to learn and whose voices are missing today.
  2. Design the approach: who to invite, where to meet, and how to make taking part easy and fair.
  3. Run listening sessions and interviews, with your staff involved where it helps.
  4. Make sense of what you heard, looking for patterns, tensions and surprises.
  5. Turn findings into programming recommendations and a plan to close the loop with participants.

Advisory and drafting

Publish a public values statement and code of conduct

Say plainly how your institution will and won't use AI, in words visitors, staff and partners can read, and build it into how people actually work.

Request a tailored proposal
Format
Working sessions and drafting, remote or on-site
Duration
3 to 5 weeks
Participants
Leadership, communications, and staff who work with AI tools or source communities
What you get
  • A public-facing values statement
  • An internal code of conduct for staff and vendors
  • A rollout plan and review date

The program includes

  1. Clarify your commitments with leadership: what you will do, what you won't, and why.
  2. Draft the public statement in plain language for visitors, funders and community partners.
  3. Translate it into a staff code of conduct with concrete dos and don'ts for everyday decisions.
  4. Test it against real scenarios from your collections, programs and communications.
  5. Plan the rollout: where it is published, how staff are briefed, and when it is reviewed.

Consulting program

Build an AI governance framework

Define the principles, roles and review steps that guide how your institution adopts AI, from collections metadata to visitor-facing tools.

Request a tailored proposal
Format
Embedded engagement, on-site and remote
Duration
6 to 10 weeks
Participants
Digital, collections, legal and visitor-experience leads
What you get
  • A framework your team can apply on a normal Tuesday
  • Named decision owners and a review process
  • A plain-language summary for your board

The program includes

  1. Map current and planned AI use across collections, visitor tools, marketing and back-office systems.
  2. Assess the risks to public trust, provenance, source communities, staff and donor privacy.
  3. Draft principles and decision rules with your leadership team, including who signs off and when.
  4. Set up a lightweight review process for new tools, vendors and pilots.
  5. Document and hand over the framework, with a plan to revisit it as regulation and practice change.

Training

AI skills training for staff

Practical, jargon-free sessions that help teams use AI tools with good judgment and know when to stop and ask.

Request a tailored proposal
Format
In-person or live online workshops
Duration
Half day or full day
Participants
Mixed teams across departments (max. 15 people)
What you get
  • A shared vocabulary and confidence with AI tools
  • Rules of thumb for everyday use
  • Clear points to escalate risky cases

The program includes

  1. What AI tools actually do and where they fail, with examples from museum work.
  2. Hands-on exercises with the tools your team already uses.
  3. Spotting risks: accuracy, bias, copyright, privacy and community protocols.
  4. Working with your policy: what is allowed, what needs review, and who to ask.
  5. A take-home guide tailored to your organization.

Workshop

Tool and vendor procurement workshop

Learn what to ask before you buy or renew an AI-enabled tool, and how to read the answers.

Request a tailored proposal
Format
Facilitated workshop with your procurement, legal and digital teams
Duration
Half day
Participants
Procurement, legal, IT and the teams requesting tools (max. 12 people)
What you get
  • A reusable vendor question checklist
  • A simple approach to comparing vendors
  • A short decision record template

The program includes

  1. Frame the decision: what problem the tool should solve, and what could go wrong.
  2. Walk through the vendor questions: data use, training on your content, security, hosting and exit terms.
  3. Read real vendor claims and terms together and spot the gaps.
  4. Score a live example from your own shortlist.
  5. Agree on your review steps and who signs off.
