A pre-launch risk review for their first visitor chatbot.

The situation

The museum was six weeks from launching a chatbot to answer visitor questions about exhibits, opening hours and accessibility. The digital team had a working prototype and a vendor contract, but nobody had decided who was accountable when the bot gave a confident wrong answer.

What I did

  1. Mapped the questions. Sorted everything the bot would be asked, from ticketing to object provenance, by what happens if the answer is wrong.
  2. Tested the prototype. Ran real visitor questions from the front desk and social channels through it, and logged every answer that was wrong, incomplete or overconfident.
  3. Read the vendor terms. Reviewed data use, retention, and whether the museum's content could be used to train the vendor's models.
  4. Wrote the safety net. Drafted a one-page escalation and correction process, and agreed which topics the bot should decline to answer.

What changed

  • The chatbot launched with a narrower scope and plain handoffs to staff for sensitive topics.
  • A named person owned corrections, with a 24-hour turnaround for wrong answers.
  • The board received a two-page summary of how the tool is governed.

We stopped asking whether the bot was good enough, and started asking what we would do when it was wrong.

Head of Digital, Science Museum

Takeaway

A chatbot is a publishing decision as much as a technology decision. The question to settle before launch is who answers for the wrong answer.
