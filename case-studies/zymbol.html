The situation

In 2012, I founded Zymbol in Boston: a twice-yearly print magazine of art and literature inspired by Symbolism and Surrealism. I saw a gap for a publication that was surreal but grounded in everyday experience, and a community of writers and artists with nowhere obvious to bring that work.

What I did

  1. Built an international network of contributors. Within two years, Zymbol had published writers and artists from more than 20 countries, alongside a reprint of Leonora Carrington in the very first issue.
  2. Brokered partnerships with artists and galleries. Partnered with Los Angeles gallery Century Guild to publish never-before-seen pages from Clive Barker’s private dream notebooks, and featured his painting on the cover of Issue 6.
  3. Funded it through the community. Ran crowdfunding campaigns built around limited-edition art prints and signed editions. Our Kickstarter for Issue 6 beat its goal with 93 backers and was selected as a Kickstarter “Project We Love.”
  4. Secured national distribution. Placed Zymbol with Barnes & Noble, Books-a-Million and independent retailers.
  5. Took it beyond the page. Released ebook editions, made the magazine available through digital library services for public libraries, ran tables at the Brooklyn Book Festival, and hosted a live reading party in Boston during the AWP writers’ conference.

What changed

  • A small independent title reached readers through major national retailers, public libraries and digital editions, with copies reaching readers on six continents.
  • Contributors went on to publish award-winning books and open solo shows.
  • Independent reviewers took notice. NewPages wrote of one issue that there was “no one piece in this issue that can be justifiably ignored.”
  • In 2017, Zymbol was acquired by a competing publisher.

Takeaway

Building an audience for adventurous work is a strategy problem, not just a creative one. You need to know who your people are, earn their trust, and make the work sustainable. That’s the same question every cultural organisation is answering now.
